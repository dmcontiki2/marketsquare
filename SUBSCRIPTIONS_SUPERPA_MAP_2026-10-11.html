3 · The Super-PA — how it works for everyone

Squire-2 already gives Pro a standing brief with rules, free data feeds, alerts and a monthly report. The Super-PA is the same engine with premium data and a specialist request form per field. It never touches money, never negotiates, never sees a seller's identity and never grants an introduction. It reports facts against the user's own rules.

Trigger classes
3
Instant — a match inside the rules (first-in alert, push + email). Daily digest — close calls, sell-side interest, price moves. Monthly — the report, even with zero matches.
What meters
Acts
Watching and reports are included; an approach (a brief sent to a seller + its Q&A) counts against 20/month, +5 for 1T. Introductions stay 1T. Premium feeds are a monthly Tuppence quote.
Data order
Free → paid
Phase 1 free feeds first (Scryfall, MTGJSON, JustTCG free, police.uk, EA flood, BoE, SARB). Paid feeds only when David has set a monthly cost; only those may be quoted.

4 · Specialist requests — how each field would set up the PA

Each card shows the request a specialist would make in his own words, the filter the PA derives, what it watches for, when it fires, when it reports, a full example report with the numbers, the risks, and what to build. A · Property investor (buy-to-let, ZA)

The request, in her words
"Find me 2–3 bedroom sectional-title flats in Pretoria East, Centurion or Midrand under R1.6m that would gross 9% or better at the going rent, built after 2005, levies under R2 500. Tell me when one appears, and tell me if my numbers are unrealistic."
Filter the PA derives
kind
buy · category Property · type Flat/Townhouse (sectional title)
area
Pretoria East, Centurion, Midrand (suburb radius from the city list)
price
≤ R1 600 000 asking; flag anything ≤ 7% under the area median
min_yield_pct
9.0 gross = (12 × achievable rent) ÷ asking
age
built ≥ 2005 (EPC/age band where available, else "not verified")
levies
≤ R2 500/month; rates and taxes recorded, not capped
exclusions
ground floor on a main road; "needs TLC"; sold-as-is deceased estates (her choice)
reach
her tier's reach (Pro = global; she set three suburbs)
What it watches for
  • New TrustSquare adverts and private-seller drafts that meet the rules — she gets in first.
  • Asking price against the area's verified sold median (Deeds data when the paid feed is on; municipal valuation roll free).
  • Achievable rent from the app's own Stays/rental adverts and the StatsSA rental index for the suburb.
  • Price drops on adverts she was shown but passed on ("close call" → now inside the rules).
  • Flood / crime / scheme-levy signals where a free source exists; otherwise "not available", never a guess.
When it triggers · when it reports
instant
an advert inside all rules, or a price drop that brings one inside
daily digest
close calls within 10% on price or yield; new comparables that move the median
monthly
report on the 1st: matches, close calls, market movement, reality check — even with zero matches
reality check
at creation and monthly: "0 of 212 flats in 90 days met 9%; 7 met 8%; relax yield to 8% or price to R1.75m"

Free data first: municipal valuation rolls (public), StatsSA rental index, SARB repo; paid Deeds feed only once ≥ 25 Pro property briefs exist.
Let an estate agency hold a sell-side brief ("tell me when a buyer brief matches my stock") — the agency pays, one agency at a time (RUL-131).

B · Mergers & acquisitions scout (small-cap, owner-managed businesses)

The request, in his words
"I buy owner-managed businesses in Gauteng and the Western Cape with EBITDA between R3m and R15m, asking at or below 4× EBITDA, in logistics, light manufacturing, facilities services or specialist retail. Owner over 58 or a stated succession reason is a plus. Alert me the day one appears; weekly on near-misses; monthly on multiples in my sectors."
Filter the PA derives
kind
buy · category Businesses (a new listing type: business-for-sale advert with anonymised financials)
geography
Gauteng, Western Cape (province-level, not suburb)
size
EBITDA R3m–R15m (seller-stated, flagged "unverified" until a data-room figure is shared after introduction)

asking ÷ EBITDA ≤ 4.0; EV = asking + stated debt − cash where given

exclusions

  • Listed-peer multiples for his sectors (JSE small-cap EV/EBITDA, free delayed data) to show whether 4× is realistic this quarter.
  • Repeat listings and price cuts on adverts he passed on.

Risks for TrustSquare

Thin market at launch: the monthly report must be useful with zero matches (sector multiples, deal flow seen) or the Tuppence subscription feels empty.
Suggestions
Add a "Business for sale" listing type (Quick: 7 cards — sector, province, revenue band, EBITDA band, asking band, reason, handover) feeding the same brief engine.
Charge the premium part as a monthly Tuppence quote (CIPC lookups + peer multiples) only after the first introduction; the free Gazette and JSE data carry the brief until then.
Let brokers and agencies hold sell-side briefs as an Agency category (business brokers), under the one-agency-at-a-time queue of RUL-131.

6 · Risks across the whole design

  • Advertising dark features on a price card — fixed today for Squire; make the plans page read only from /pricing/ladder so a static line can never drift again.
  • Ruling-number collisions between branches and main (RUL-220/221 happened today) — reserve a number block per branch before a build run.
  • Uncommitted branch work — Squire-2 Phase 1 sat uncommitted for a day because a worktree pointed at a dead session path; the nightly checkpoint should commit every worktree, not only main.
  • Premium-feed quotes with no matches can read as paying for nothing; the monthly report with close calls and market movement is the answer David already approved — it must never be skipped.
  • FAIS and "advice": reports print facts against the user's own rules with sources and dates; the verdict names rules, never merits; no "buy / sell" wording anywhere.
  • Anonymity under pressure: an M&A or bullion brief tempts richer seller detail before acceptance; Mode B stays absolute — the brief describes the need, never the person.
