## 2026-09-14 — RIA-DISCUSSION-1: the redundancy / independence / accountability discussion paper (RUL-134)

David, after the launch post-mortem: *"we are not technologically there yet, the science is there
but we need to design redundancy, independence, and accountability into the project"* — and then
*"it is to be done, not maybe. Please set this discussion up with your suggestions as input."*

Produced as Claude's CTO input, not a design:

- **Word paper** in Professional Navy house style: `Baseline Readiness - Redundancy, Independence,
  Accountability - Discussion Input 2026-09-14 - nice.docx`, regenerated by
  `scripts/build_ria_discussion.py` so the argument lives in git rather than in a binary.
- **Visual companion** `genie/BASELINE_READINESS_RIA.html`, indexed into the Visuals gallery.
  Colour is tied to function: three pillar hues from a palette validated on a white surface
  (lightness, chroma, CVD ΔE 13.1 deutan / 20.8 tritan, normal-vision ΔE 21.7, contrast — all
  pass), status colours reserved and never reused as a pillar hue, and every status also carries
  a word so nothing reads by colour alone.

**The argument, in one line:** every instrument measured our own machinery rather than a stranger's
experience — a test our code passes proves the code ran, never that a stranger succeeded.

**Three patterns behind eight findings:** self-reference (our fixtures, our documents, our logs);
shared assumption (the checker inherits the blind spot — the agreement tool compared three copies
to each other and never the header to the footer); absence read as health (a blank panel, a missing
file and a quiet log all rendering green).

**Six proposed gate conditions**, none satisfiable by our own machinery agreeing with itself.
Honest scoring today: two FAIL, three UNTESTED, one PASS. The cheapest is condition 5 — prove every
instrument fires against a deliberately broken case — which already exists in this project's
practice and would have caught most of the eight.

**The Council is separated by ACCESS, not vendor:** the Peer Auditor forms its verdict from outside
evidence only, because four opinions reading the same repository is a chorus. Claude sees everything
and therefore may not be the auditor.

**Five decisions left to David** and deliberately unanswered — scope, force of a red verdict, drill
window, budget ceiling for worker APIs, and whether a real human stranger must complete the journey
per baseline. Answering them here would put Claude's guess into canon; RUL-037 delegates technical
calls, and this is the specification layer that does the delegating.

Risks named in the paper rather than left implicit: coordination cost on a solo founder,
independence being lost quietly the moment the auditor is handed our summary, the auditor looking
less productive while being more valuable, Rule B7's cost ceiling needed before the first worker key
is issued, and the largest single point of failure being one person.
