Where it dies, in order
Letter → click. 22% open and 0.08% click as a human — the letter is read and the ask inside it does not move anyone, the same verdict on 6, 11 and 14 Sep.
Click → door. 13 of 16 door sessions landed within minutes of the 00:10 send and lasted a second — scanners and link previews, which the funnel used to count as people.
Door → account. The one human finished with a score of 100 and was handed over to become an account, and did not.
Shelf → introduction. No stranger has ever paid 1 Tuppence, so the assumption the whole model rests on is still untested after 17 days.

Failure mode 3 — friction Three laps of features → legal → scorecard is a person who cannot see the exit, and the ten silent minutes before it fit someone reading terms on a phone.
The seam took his work and asked for more He had given a photo, a category and eighteen minutes, and the hand-over answered with an account to create before anything of his could go live.
The other fifteen sessions Thirteen lasted a second or less — most of them within minutes of the 00:10 send — and never moved past "landed" or "photos"; two were your own phone and laptop.
One session is a fact, not a trend; it is the only measured evidence the launch has produced of a stranger meeting the design, which is why it is on its own board.
