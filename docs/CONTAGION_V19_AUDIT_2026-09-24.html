Contagion Model v1.9 Audit

The growth simulation re-based on the casual-worker research: a bigger prospect group, the referral component, the publish-key gate, the new reach channels — and what three weeks of real numbers say about the model.

The crux: the casual-worker market is about 3.5× bigger than the model assumed on 19 Sep, and the referral component is real — but one variable decides whether any of it happens: the publish key. With the e-mail key kept (RUL-166, yesterday), the lane never ignites: 1,653 service workers listed after three years in the mid run. With a phone or link key from week 6 it becomes the largest lane in the model — 99,771 with the key alone, 108,319 with ranks, radio and SA Youth added. Either way it is slow money: 209 workers by March 2027 in the best plan, and the household's paid introduction — not her subscription — is where the revenue is. Reality check three weeks in: the model expected 67 sellers by now, 40 of them from letters; the letters have produced 0. The model is updated to v1.9 and deployed; two calibration decisions are yours below.

Read the band, not the line

The medians of the e-mail-key runs look respectable at year 3 because a third of the sampled worlds assume most casual workers can finish an e-mail step (svcMail up to .60). The mid run uses the middle guess (.35). The first week of door beacons will replace that guess with a measurement — it is the single cheapest number to learn.

Untouched, on purpose

Employer yes-rate (.20), reach of a roster (.60), publish rate at the door (svcAct .12/week), households per worker (3) and the share who confirm (.40), churn, introductions per worker — no new evidence arrived, so no number moved. Every one is marked guess and sampled in the band.

Behind the toggle, so nothing else moved

All of this sits inside the Services-lane switch. Lane OFF reproduces the 19 Sep model draw for draw (checked headless: 91 / 150,917 / 168,093 sellers at weeks 8 / 52 / 156, the numbers the dashboard pins), so the BEAT THE MODEL card does not move.

Pinning the measured click rate removes the letter lane's contribution almost entirely; the year-1 curve still stands on organic word of mouth (peers × tell × take), which nothing has measured yet — the first ten self-published sellers' ?src= codes will.
What the three weeks prove

The letter lane runs about 40× below the model: 2 human clicks from 2,491 letters, one draft, nothing published. The model's open rate (21.8%) and bounce rate are pinned to data already; click is not, because two clicks was not a rate. It is still two clicks — but from 534 openers now, not 155.

What they do not prove

The organic engine (a seller tells peers, some sign up) has had 40 seeded sellers and three weeks; the model itself expected only a handful of organic sign-ups in that time, so zero is not yet a contradiction. It becomes one by week 8.

5 · Decisions — one sentence each

D2 · the publish key: allow a phone number (one-time code) or the draft link itself as the account key for casual workers, EULA sign-off still mandatory in the app? decision

Yes turns 1,653 into 99,771 workers by year 3 in the mid run; no keeps RUL-166 exactly as worded and the lane stays a trickle.

Pin the measured click rate (0.37% of openers) into the dashboard's model, knowing the BEAT THE MODEL card will drop? decision

It is the honest number today; the card exists to be beaten by reality, not to flatter the plan.

Start the ranks / radio / SA Youth channels at model week 8 (mid-November) or only after the key ships? decision

The model says channels without the key add little; with the key they add about 9% — the money in them (posters R500, ambassador R20–30 per advert, radio R1–5k) can wait for the first ?src= counts.
