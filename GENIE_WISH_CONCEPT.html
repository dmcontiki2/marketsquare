The decisions I made building it (yours to overrule)

1The full ceremony plays once, not every time. Smoke, lamp, genie rising, the line spoken — that is the first time a person ever opens the app, and it is wonderful exactly once. After that the lamp sits quietly in the corner and glows. Tap it and the genie leans in without the theatre. A three-second film in front of a shop you visit daily becomes a locked door by the fourth day.

2He greets you, then gets out of the way. The arrival is the one full-screen moment your own covering rule allows, and only because nothing is chosen yet. The instant you say your wish, the genie shrinks to the corner and everything he says appears in a band that pushes the results down rather than sitting on top of them. Search, chips, Filter, Sort, Trust and the bottom bar stay live throughout. The meter under the phone is watching for exactly this and says no.

3Selling is the wish worth granting first. Finding things has a fallback — people scroll, and browsing is pleasant. Filling in a form has no fallback: they abandon it and you get nothing. 546 people were emailed, 61 clicked, 0 published. A genie that turns one sentence into a finished advert attacks the number that is actually zero. The search genie stays parked where you left it.

4He never publishes anything on his own. He drafts. You read it and press Publish. Anything he could not work out he shows in amber and asks for — he never invents a price, a year or a condition. A confident wrong advert costs a seller more than an empty form.

5Voice out, typing in — for now. He speaks his greeting aloud (your phone's own voice, nothing bought). Listening to speech is a different bill and a permissions prompt, so version one reads what you type. The wish box is one line and takes a scruffy sentence.

6He is drawn, not filmed. Everything you see is code — no video file, no image, nothing to download. It costs the app nothing to load and it looks the same on a cheap phone.

What this would cost to run

One short AI call per wish. The sentence in, a structured answer out. Roughly the same size as the advert-writing call the app already makes today, so it sits inside the daily AI ceiling you already set rather than needing a new one.

The honest limits: he can only offer categories that actually have stock, or the ring advertises an empty shop — that was the reason for parking the search half, and it still holds. Selling has no such problem: a person listing a bakkie does not care how many bakkies are already there.

If you like it, the next step

This is a picture, not a feature. Nothing is wired into the app, no flag, no deploy. To make it real: the wish box calls the AI with the category list and the field names, the answer fills the existing listing form, and the person presses Publish. The form already exists and already validates — the genie just arrives with it filled in.

Say the word and I write it up against the Zoom spec the way the search half was written up, so the two halves stay one design and not two.
