Zoom on a phone

Same engine as the desktop prototype — literally the same file, extracted — in the shell it actually has to live in. 378 × 800, one thumb. Tap the phone; it is live.

The question moved to the bottomOn desktop the question sits above the results. On a phone that puts every tap target at the top of a 6-inch screen — the furthest point from the thumb. The question is now a bottom sheet: options land in the lower 45% where the thumb rests.
Results never disappearDrag the handle down and the sheet peeks — question title only, one tap to reopen. The funnel is never a wizard that hides what you are choosing between.
Chips scroll sideways, never wrapFour chips would eat three lines of a phone screen if they wrapped. One line, horizontal scroll, newest chip pinned into view. The geo chip stays a single deepening chip — Pretoria › Menlo Park › 12th Street — not three.
Two options per row, 56px tallComfortably above the 44px touch minimum, count on every one. Six shown, the long tail folded behind search — a phone must never render a 40-item facet list.
Arrival gives the screen backOnce it fits, the sheet collapses to a slim green bar — taps used, matches found, one widen button — and the results own the display.
What got cut for the phoneThe side panel is explanation for you, not product: the rules list, the tap budget, the saved-watch list. On the phone, search became one icon, the category rail became a sheet behind the title, and Watches move to the profile tab. Four things survive on screen — chips, count, results, question. Everything else is a tap away.
