# Buzz — EULA clause, DRAFT for the next EULA revision
**Status: DRAFT. Not in the live EULA. Routed to the counsel/EULA revision track (v1.9 line), not
applied unilaterally, because the EULA has an external review track and Claude does not edit it.**

David, 14 Sep 2026, asked for Buzz to be provided "voetstoots … no complaints allowed … switch it
off switch for both parties, and included as such in the EULA".

## Three corrections carried into the draft, and why

1. **"Voetstoots" is not the instrument.** It is a sale-of-goods term excluding liability for
   *latent defects in a thing sold*; Buzz is a free service attached to a platform. It has also
   been largely displaced in consumer dealings: CPA s55/s56 give a right to services of reasonable
   quality that cannot be contracted out of. What does the job is a plain as-is description plus a
   limitation of liability.
2. **"No complaints allowed" cannot be written.** Nobody can be contracted out of complaining — to
   TrustSquare, to the National Consumer Commission, or to a court. The enforceable and useful half
   is that TrustSquare does not mediate what two people said to each other.
3. **It must be drawn to attention, not buried.** CPA s49 requires a term that limits liability or
   assumes risk to be conspicuous, in plain language, and acknowledged. *Van Wyk t/a Skydive Mossel
   Bay v UPS SCS South Africa* (2020, WCC) is the case that shows a clause buried in standard terms
   fails that test. **This is why the same words are already on screen at the moment somebody
   switches Buzz on** (built 14 Sep, `ms.js` → `buzzRender`), and the paragraph below is the full
   version for the EULA. The two must not drift apart.

## The clause, as drafted

> ### Buzz — a convenience, not a communications service
>
> Buzz lets two people who are already connected on TrustSquare send each other one short line. It
> is free and is provided as it stands.
>
> **It is not a guarantee of delivery.** A buzz is sent as a push notification and falls back to
> email. Phones are switched off, batteries go flat and networks fail. If something matters,
> telephone the person — you have each other's contact details, and Buzz is only the convenient
> version of a call you could already make.
>
> **TrustSquare does not read, moderate or mediate.** We take no position on what two people say to
> each other and we do not settle disputes between them.
>
> **Either of you can close it at any time.** Closing Buzz closes it in both directions, we tell the
> other person plainly that it is closed, and whoever closed it is the one who can open it again.
>
> **Liability.** To the fullest extent the law allows, TrustSquare is not liable for loss arising
> from a buzz that was delayed, undelivered or never sent — including lost work, lost income or a
> missed appointment. Nothing in this clause limits liability for gross negligence or intentional
> wrongdoing, or any liability that cannot lawfully be excluded under the Consumer Protection Act
> 68 of 2008.

## One note for whoever lands this

A wall of exclusions inside a product whose whole promise is trust costs more than it saves, and
s49's plain-language requirement pushes the same way. The clause is written to read as an honest
description of what Buzz is. Keep it that way.
