1  The seam register

Class is set by what a wrong answer costs, not by how clever the model is. RED means a refund cannot undo it.

RED — irreversible harm to a person or to the trust promise AMBER — money, ranking or reputation; recoverable at a cost in trust GREEN — cosmetic or retryable

3  Pre-design rules

Canon. human_seam_check.py fails if any of these six goes missing from the register.

PR-1

PARALLEL RUN, NEVER A SWITCH-OVER

No AI surface replaces a working mechanism without both running side by side and their outputs compared under real load. Applies most directly to HS-13 (deterministic matcher -> AI). This is the single rule that would have saved both companies in the video.

PR-2

THE SEAM IS BUILT EMPTY

Every RED and AMBER surface declares its human role and its measurable trigger BEFORE a human exists. Klarna had a bench to rehire; MarketSquare has none, so the seam must be pre-cut.

PR-3

NEVER REMOVE THE FALLBACK AND ADD THE AI IN THE SAME CHANGE

Yum mandated Dragon Tail AND centralised the DoorDash contract simultaneously, so the franchise lost the AI's replacement and its own manual tools at once. One change at a time; the old lane stays until the new one has proven itself in production.

PR-4

RUN THE DRAGON TAIL TEST ON EVERY NEW INFORMATION SURFACE

Before exposing any platform-internal signal to sellers, buyers, agencies or affiliates, write down what a purely self-interested actor does with it. If the answer is unknown, it is not ready to ship.

PR-5

NO PUBLIC CLAIM THE MACHINERY CANNOT YET HONOUR

Klarna's real loss was narrative: it went to the public market on an AI-first thesis and reversed within ten weeks. MarketSquare's copy must never promise a capability that is currently a hope. Vendor-neutral AI copy (RG-0035) is the same discipline -- extend it to capability claims.

PR-6

HIRING HUMANS IS A PLANNED MILESTONE, NOT A RETREAT

Because MarketSquare is AI-first BY DESIGN rather than by cost-cutting, adding humans at a declared trigger is the design working, not the design failing. Say so in advance, publicly and internally, so the first hire never reads as a Klarna-style U-turn.
