What has gone wrong since soft launch — and what it was

29 August to 6 September 2026 · every item raised in the daily watch register and the regression ledger, placed on the day it was found. Built from the registers on disk, not from memory.
Failure Something did not work. The product, a customer's path through it, or the safety net that watches it was actually broken.
Nonconformance A departure from our own rules or design. Usually an instrument reporting the wrong thing, or a ruling not followed. The product mostly still worked.
Observation Noticed and written down. Not a defect yet — environment noise, debt, or something that will matter later.

How to read the shape of this. Thirty-two items in nine days looks alarming until you separate them. Half are orange — instruments telling us the wrong thing rather than the product failing. That is a finding about the watchers, not about the app.

Almost all of it was found by us, not by users. One item came from a customer path at all, and that one — the support form — is the exception that matters: it was swallowing messages silently, which is precisely why nobody complained about it.

The dangerous pattern is not the count, it is the repetition. The integrity baseline went stale four times. A locked check read a false red four times. Three separate surfaces reported "configured" as though it meant "working". Each was fixed at the class in the end, which is why the repeats stop rather than continue.

The classification is mine, not a field in the data. The registers record severity, not category. Where an item could sit in two colours I have put it in the more serious one — a credential exposure is red even though nothing broke, because the remediation is real.
