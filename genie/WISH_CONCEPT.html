The decisions I made building it (yours to overrule)

1The full ceremony plays once, not every time. Smoke, lamp, genie rising, the line spoken — that is the first time a person ever opens the app, and it is wonderful exactly once. After that the lamp sits quietly in the corner and glows. One tap and the genie leans in without the theatre. A three-second film in front of a shop you visit daily becomes a locked door by the fourth day.

2He greets you, then gets out of the way. The arrival is the one full-screen moment your own covering rule allows, and only because nothing is chosen yet. The instant you say your wish, the genie shrinks to the corner and everything he says appears in a band that pushes the results down rather than sitting on top of them. And during the arrival itself he simply speaks — no speech bubble, nothing written over the painting (your call, 7 September). Text appears only if the sound is off, so a person who cannot hear is still told what to do. Search, chips, Filter, Sort, Trust and the bottom bar stay live throughout. The meter under the phone is watching for exactly this and says no.

3Selling is the wish worth granting first. Finding things has a fallback — people scroll, and browsing is pleasant. Filling in a form has no fallback: they abandon it and you get nothing. 546 people were emailed, 61 clicked, 0 published. A genie that turns one sentence into a finished advert attacks the number that is actually zero. The search genie stays parked where you left it.

4He never publishes anything on his own. He drafts. You read it and press Publish. Anything he could not work out he shows in amber and asks for — he never invents a price, a year or a condition. A confident wrong advert costs a seller more than an empty form.

5Voice out, typing in — and the voice needs buying. You said he sounded tin-like. You were hearing a mistake of mine: I had dropped the pitch to make him deep, and browsers resample badly that low, so what came out was the artefacts. Pitch is back near normal and he now picks the best natural voice your device has — which is why he sounds different on your phone and your laptop. That is still the free voice, and it will never sound like a genie. The real answer is cheap: he says one line, so it is one recorded audio file, generated once from a proper voice service and shipped with the app. No per-use cost, no monthly bill, and it sounds the same on every device. Listening to speech is a different matter — a real bill and a microphone permission — so version one reads what you type.

6Two paintings, not a film. The night and the arrival are two still images that cross-fade — about 45KB each, which is less than one photograph on a listing. A video would be ten times that and would stutter on a cheap phone. The art here is your own Grok pair, used as a placeholder so you can judge the idea at the right quality. Ours gets generated properly — see the note below.

What this would cost to run

One short AI call per wish. The sentence in, a structured answer out. Roughly the same size as the advert-writing call the app already makes today, so it sits inside the daily AI ceiling you already set rather than needing a new one.

The honest limits: he can only offer categories that actually have stock, or the ring advertises an empty shop — that was the reason for parking the search half, and it still holds. Selling has no such problem: a person listing a bakkie does not care how many bakkies are already there.

The two prompts, ready to run

1 · The night. Vertical 9:16 fantasy oil painting. A deep desert night, indigo sky crowded with stars and a faint violet Milky Way, slender crescent moon high on the left. Moonlit dunes rolling to the horizon. Far off, the warm golden silhouette of a domed Arabian city with minarets. Lower centre, resting on the sand, an ornate antique brass lamp with engraved filigree and a blue jewel, catching warm light, one thin curl of glowing golden smoke starting from its spout. Cinematic, painterly, rich. No text, no watermark, no lettering.

2 · The arrival. The same painting and the same lamp in the same place, but now a powerful genie has risen from the spout on a thick ribbon of luminous magenta, violet and cyan smoke. Teal-blue skin, muscular chest and shoulders, arms folded across his chest, gold armbands and hoop earrings, magenta and gold embroidered turban with a sapphire brooch, neat dark beard, a confident half smile. Waist down he dissolves into the coloured smoke. Same palette, same night, same city on the horizon. No text, no watermark.

Where this lives now

It is its own sub-project: MarketSquare\genie\. Both halves sit together — WISH_CONCEPT.html (this one, selling and finding from a sentence) and SEARCH_CONCEPT.html (the 4 September one you already ruled on), plus the artwork in genie\art\. They belong in one folder because they are one design; splitting them is how a thing gets built twice.

Nothing about the launch changes. This is not in the launch month and is not on the roadmap — it is a folder that is ready when you are.

If you like it, the next step

This is a picture, not a feature. Nothing is wired into the app, no flag, no deploy. To make it real: the wish box calls the AI with the category list and the field names, the answer fills the existing listing form, and the person presses Publish. The form already exists and already validates — the genie just arrives with it filled in.

Say the word and I write it up against the Zoom spec the way the search half was written up, so the two halves stay one design and not two.
