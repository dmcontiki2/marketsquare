The spreader — built, and what it does differently

MarketSquare · 14 September 2026 · running in HARNESS.html, walked headless across all eight categories

What changed since the design. The design said three modes, one engine, and one place that knows a mode exists. That is now literally true: finish() is five lines long and it is the only function in the file that asks which mode you are in. Everything before it — the door, the four taps, the category table, the week grid — runs identically for a stranger listing a room and a collector checking a card, and none of it was touched. The two listing tails differ inside drawDraft() by one variable; the looking tail is its own screen because it answers a different question, not because it is a different app.

The three tails Everything above these screens is the same code, the same taps, the same table.

Stranger, listing

7–8 steps · the only path that asks anything

Full coaching, all three of his own scores, the credential list, the price note. He has never seen any of it. Nothing is collected on the way in — he taps four times and sees his own advert before he is asked for a thing.

… and the one ask, last

After he has seen what he gets

One email line, and the terms — including Buzz exactly as it is, a doorbell with an off switch on both sides. This is the only thing the spreader ever asks. Once through it he is a member and is never asked again, on any device he is signed in on.

Member, listing

5–6 steps · nothing re-asked, ever

Publishing as him, terms already accepted, and one line where the lecture was: the score that moved and the points still on the table. Teaching a man twice is how you lose him. Publish goes straight through — no gate, no email, no consent screen.

Looking — the competition-floor case “Is it there?” is one question and it deserves one answer.

Five answers, and then stop

4–5 steps · member or stranger, identical

No narrowing, no sort, no paging, no map. If the right one is not in five, the question was wrong, not the list. Tapping one hands over to the app, where the introduction and the Tuppence live — the spreader never holds either.

Ask your traders

Maurice’s move, in the app

The people he has dealt with before, five at most, one line typed once. It is not a broadcast: it sends N individual buzzes, each still governed by that pair’s own switch, that pair’s closed state and his own hourly limit. Nothing about consent forks.

Replies come back one to one

Never a group thread

Whoever knows something buzzes him back, on his own. A group thread is where moderation, support load and “who said that” begin, and none of those belong in a doorbell. That is the one line I would not cross without you ruling it.

Why that is only three facts. A stateless snippet stays simple at any size; the moment it has to KNOW things it stops behaving like one. So the cap is on knowledge, not on features — and three facts, fetched in one call the browser can make because it is the same origin, is not complexity. Branches are. Eight categories × two directions × three modes is forty-eight screens if you write them; it is one renderer, one table and one finish() if you do not.
The caps, and why each one is load-bearing
  • Five answers in the lookup — a sixth turns an answer into a shelf, and the shelf is the app.
  • Five traders in the phone-around — Maurice phones three or four people, not thirty. Six is a mailing list.
  • One line, 120 characters — unchanged from Buzz. There is no thread to scroll.
  • Individual buzzes, never a broadcast — consent does not fork, and every recipient keeps his own off switch.
  • One ask, at the end, once — and the spreader never holds Tuppence, an introduction or a payment.
