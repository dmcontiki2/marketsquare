How an advert gets its second language

1. She writes it in her language. Quick now writes her advert in that language too, and records which one it is.
2. In the app, on her advert's edit screen, she picks one extra language from her country's list.
3. She sees the translation and the same text translated back into her own words, then taps Approve.
4. Buyers who read that language see her approved version. Everyone else sees the original with a code chip.
5. An English search layer means an isiZulu advert still turns up in an English search.

My advice for easy, quick, fluid — and stunning

  • FIRSTMake Save publish in one step, with the email she types at the end and the terms accepted in that same tap, because every screen after "Save" loses people.
  • NEXTKeep one door. When you approve the preview, the email links move to it and the heavy old door retires.
  • NEXTTranslate the draft screen's own words for all five languages, so the last screen feels as much hers as the first.
  • LOOKReal local photos for places (a Mamelodi street, the Menlyn skyline) would make the area step beautiful without it reading as a room.
  • LOOKOne small celebration when the advert is saved (her photo and title rising into a card) would make the ending feel like an arrival.
