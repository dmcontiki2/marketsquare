Buzz — what it costs if it escalates

MarketSquare · 14 September 2026 · Hetzner CPX22: 3 vCPU, 4 GB, 80 GB disk, 20 TB traffic

Bandwidth and CPU are not the question. At half a million active pairs Buzz uses 37 GB a month against 20 000 GB included — under 0.2%. A buzz is a few hundred bytes in and one small encrypted payload out: no page render, no image, no query of any size.
The ceiling is worker threads — and that is exactly how it would have hurt the app. /buzz runs on the same ~40 shared threads that serve listing pages. A slow push vendor would not have made Buzz slow; it would have made the site slow. That was a real fault, found by asking this question, and it is fixed.

Two a second is inside the peak for a modest userbase — which is why this was worth fixing before anyone used it.

The three changes

Push capped — BUZZ_PUSH_TIMEOUT = 2: a buzz may hold a worker thread for two seconds, not eight. The intro-reminder lane keeps its 8 s default; the timeout became a parameter rather than a global change.

Email off the request path — queued with BackgroundTasks. The sender is told immediately that it is going by email; the request never waits on a mail provider.

Retention on the only unbounded thing — BUZZ_LOG_KEEP_DAYS = 90, pruned lazily on ~1 send in 500. The log is operational — rate limiting and did-it-arrive — never a conversation, so ageing it out costs nothing. Without it: 23 GB a year on an 80 GB disk.

Watched, not assumed Sustained above ~10 buzzes/sec (roughly 350 000 active pairs) → move the push off the request path too, exactly as the email now is; cheap when needed, unnecessary before. buzz_log above ~2 GB → shorten the retention, not the feature. And noted honestly: database.py sets no busy_timeout, so write contention surfaces as an immediate "database is locked" rather than a short wait — pre-existing and app-wide, not caused by Buzz, but it adds writes, and it is a one-line hardening whenever that file is next open.
