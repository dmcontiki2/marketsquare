What the Genie actually costs — on David's own model setup

The harness is now chosen. After his own investigation across many harness courses, David picked DeepSeek Harness — plug and play, no foundation bias, anything that does not work gets replaced, free and MIT. It installs on the Hetzner server and its plug-ins are actual API models. His three subscriptions are a separate thing — his own bench, not app plumbing. Rev 1 of this page assumed "open source" meant renting a graphics card and rev 2 assumed the harness lived on his PC; both were my assumptions and both are gone. The arithmetic survived every correction, and it is better on his design than on the lane the app runs today.

Separate from all of it — your own bench. Claude as the backbone, OpenAI as an independent test ground and auditor, Grok for video, graphics and security. Those are the tools you work with. They never sit in a visitor's request path, so they carry no per-user cost and appear nowhere in the figures on this page.

This is the part I had wrong, and it is the best thing about your design. I costed the Genie as if every trip had to be written while somebody watched a spinner. Most of it does not. There are only so many trips. Twelve are already built. A hundred, written in batches by the harness on a queue when nothing is waiting, would answer almost anything anyone asks in the first year — and the live call shrinks to the small one that reads the sentence and picks the right answer.

That call costs a fifth of a cent on DeepSeek, or nothing on a free lane. The bill you were protecting against largely stops existing.

What is still worth watching

I could not confirm that Scaleway carries DeepSeek. Their model-list page did not render for me, and a provider comparison describes Scaleway as hosting Llama 3.3 and other open-weight models with no DeepSeek mentioned. The prices in the table above are DeepSeek's own published rates, not Scaleway's. You hold that account — quicker for you to look than for me to guess. It matters beyond price: you picked Scaleway for European hosting, and DeepSeek's own API is not that. The input here is sentences people type about their own lives.
The harness goes behind the seam, not instead of it. The app keeps asking ai_provider for a task tier; DeepSeek Harness becomes one more adapter beside the three already there. That is the whole reason your cap keeps working — one place that meters, one spend log, one price card. A harness metering its own plug-ins would give you three ceilings that cannot see each other, and three lanes each inside their own limit can still total past yours.
Its event log is a free second opinion on cost. The harness keeps an append-only record of every call including token counts. The app's own spend log records the lane it meant to use, which is a known weak spot; the harness records the one that actually answered. Cross-check the two and that gap closes without new code.
Pin the version. It is at v0.1. Interfaces at that number move, and a harness that updates itself in the live path is a lane change nobody reviewed. Pin it, read the notes, move on purpose.
n8n stays what it is. Workflow automation, in Docker, on the same box. It does not become the model router and the two do not merge — but they do share the CPUs, so the batch queue still wants a quiet window.
Ahead-of-time work and live requests share the same CPUs. The harness holds no model, so it is light — but a queue of a hundred trip write-ups is a hundred outbound calls with retries, on the box that is also serving the site. Give the batch queue a lower priority and a window when nobody is browsing, and this never becomes a symptom.

The appasks for a task tier — "triage", "haiku", "design" — never a model name.

The lanesopenai → anthropic → scaleway today. DeepSeek is one more adapter and one registry line.

Your 14 August ruling sorted the lanes by role rather than by vendor — base, auto-failover, safety net — and the seam already maps an abstract task tier onto whatever the active lane uses. So putting a cheap capped lane at the base is the same shape of change as the three already there, and every cap and spend log you already built keeps working through it.

Two additions I would make to that ruling, both small and both about caps, since capping is your stated condition: a free lane is base for the free conversation only and fails over to a paid lane, never the other way round — a capped lane must never stand between a paying customer and the thing they paid for. And the design tier gets its own monthly ceiling, separate from the rest, because it is the only tier where one call can cost more than a hundred of the others.

What this does to the stage order

The narrow front door costs nothing per question at all. The prototype built today routes a sentence with plain code and makes no AI call. Zero, at any volume, with no lane and no ceiling.
The wide version, on your setup, is cents. Under a cent for anyone who buys; a fifth of a cent for anyone who does not; nothing at all for the first 740 a day.
Your instinct on models was right and mine was wrong. You said the free and open route would make it not more expensive. On the numbers it does — it takes the one genuinely expensive call and makes it 98% cheaper, or removes it from the live path entirely.

My call, in one line: cost is no longer a reason to hold this to Stage 5 — but that only removes a reason, it does not make the case for moving it. Stages 1 and 2 are about whether anyone lists at all, which is a different question from what a feature costs, and the stage order is yours.

How these numbers were reached. Per-call costs use the app's own worst-case envelopes from AI_BASELINE.json v2.0 — triage 2 500 in / 400 out, haiku 4 000 / 1 800, design 12 000 / 4 000 — priced on each lane. Base lane figures are the card's own: triage $0.00098, haiku $0.00296, design $0.18. DeepSeek is priced at its published 31 July 2026 rates: V4 Flash $0.14 in / $0.28 out per million tokens, V4 Pro $0.435 / $0.87. DeepSeek has signalled a broader price rise without publishing replacement rates, so these should be re-checked before anything depends on them. A conversation is five calls; a dossier adds one design-tier call. Tuppence is $2 fixed (PRICING_CANON §4), so 5T is $10. Free-lane allowances — Groq 1 000 requests a day, Cerebras ~1M tokens a day, OpenRouter 1 000 a day after a one-off $10 — were read from published comparisons on 9 Sep 2026; Google AI Studio and Mistral's free tiers are excluded because both use prompts for training.
