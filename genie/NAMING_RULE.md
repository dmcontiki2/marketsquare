# Naming rule for every example, mock-up, demo and test fixture

**David, 14 September 2026:** *"Dont name the employer as one race and then the employee as
another, always use the same race type of names for both please — this is an important trigger in
some countries."*

## The rule

**Both sides of a relationship get names from the SAME naming tradition.** Worker and employer,
buyer and seller, tenant and landlord, candidate and company — whenever two people appear in a
worked example and one of them has power over the other, their names must not encode a racial
hierarchy. In South Africa that pairing is not a neutral detail; it is a trigger, and a product
that ships it is making a statement it never meant to make.

## What was wrong, and what it is now

Every housekeeper in the examples was **Thandi / Grace / Joseph** and every employer was
**Mrs van Wyk**. Fixed on 14 Sep by making the employer **Mrs Nkosi** — the worker names were left
alone, so both sides now sit in the same tradition. Changed in: `genie/HARNESS.html`,
`genie/bots/homehelp/BOT_HOMEHELP.html`, `genie/bots/README.md`, `genie/QUICKLIST_DESIGN_NOTES.md`,
and the two lines in `CHANGELOG.md` that quote the same example sentence.

## What this rule does NOT mean

It does not mean one tradition for everything. A **set of peers in the same role** should be as
varied as the country is — the agency bulk-import examples in `ms.js` (Pieter, Nomsa, Themba,
Sipho, Thandi, Lisa, Ann) are seven business owners of equal standing, and that variety is right
and stays. The rule is about PAIRS, and specifically about pairs with a power gradient between them.

## When the tradition is a choice

Either tradition is fine so long as it is the same on both sides. If a future example needs a
different one, change both names together, never one.
