You are right, and the code already agrees with you

I went and read the app before building this. There is already a Listing Rating in TrustSquare — a real 100-point score with a real 50-point publish bar — and it already returns a list of what to fix, biggest win first. Nobody ever shows it to a seller conversationally. That is the whole opportunity in one sentence.

What the score is actually made of

WhatPointsThe rule, exactly as the code has it
Photosup to 40 First photo 10, then +8 each, capped at 40. So five photos maxes it — and “two more photos” is literally +16.
The facts about the thing50 Split evenly across the fields that category needs, plus a description of 15+ words. A car needs make, model, year, mileage, transmission — six slots, so 8.3 each. A house needs type, beds, baths, to-let-or-sell — five slots, 10 each.
A real price6 “POA” scores zero. Naming a number is worth 6 points.
Suburb4Where the thing is.

Your three examples, checked against the code

What you said the genie should sayVerdict
“You should have two more photos to improve your Listing Rating score” Real
Exactly +16. And the app already has room-by-room prompts — for a car it wants front, rear, both sides, interior, dashboard with the odometer visible, engine bay, boot, and any damage. The genie can just read those out.
“We need the car’s model to get the specifications, John” Real
Model is one of the six required car fields, worth 8.3 on its own — and it is the key that unlocks the specifications the app already fetches. Your instinct that it does double duty is right.
“Do you have plans for the house?” Finds a hole
Plans score nothing today. But look at what the app already asks collectors for: an authentication certificate. And sellers: a trade certificate. The wizard already believes in proof documents — the score just ignores them. That is a gap your question found, and it is the most valuable of the three.

What I would do about the third one

Not squeeze plans into the rating. Documents are a different animal: photos show what a thing looks like, documents prove what it is. Approved building plans, a service history, a certificate of authenticity — those belong next to the Trust Score, not the Listing Rating, because they are evidence about the seller’s claim.

That is a decision for you rather than me — it touches what the Trust Score means, which is the heart of the product. I have written it up rather than quietly changing it.

Something the test found, which you should know about

Running the conversation end to end threw up a real one. Answer every question about the car, give a price, a suburb and a decent description — and add no photographs at all — and the listing scores 60 out of 100 and is allowed to publish.

The arithmetic is right: the facts are worth 50, the price 6, the suburb 4. Photos are worth 40, and nothing says you must have any of them. So a listing with no picture clears a bar set at 50, comfortably.

A listing with no photograph helps nobody — not the buyer, not the seller, and not the marketplace that has to look worth visiting. I would put a floor under it: at least one photo before anything publishes, which the guided wizard effectively achieves by asking for pictures first, but the score alone does not require.

Flagged, not changed. It touches the publish gate, and quietly moving a gate during launch month is not a thing I will do without you saying so.

Why this BOT and not the Collector

Last night I argued Collector proves it and Seller banks it. You have made the better version of that argument: there are no products because we are complex. The rating is the proof — a seller who abandons the form leaves at maybe 20 out of 100, and a listing at 20 helps nobody.

A form asks you to fill in fields. This asks one question at a time, in the order that gains the most points, tells you what each answer is worth, and stops the moment you can publish — then offers you more if you want a better listing rather than demanding it.

One rule I built in and would defend: it tells you the truth about where you are. At 52 it says “you can publish now” even though three more photos would take you to 76. A coach that will not let you finish is just a form with a voice.

Where this stands

Nothing wired, no flag, no deploy, not launch scope. Two BOTs now exist as working pictures: the Collector and this one.

The scoring here is a faithful copy for the prototype. If this gets built, the BOT calls the server for the score rather than keeping its own — one scorer, not two, the same rule that keeps the genie from becoming a second search engine.
