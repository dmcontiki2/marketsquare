The Eureka is real, and it is cheaper than you think

I checked the live site before answering rather than guessing. TrustSquare is already an installable app. The app description file is live and correctly served, and all three icons are there. Anyone can put it on their home screen today and it opens like an app.

Which means a BOT is not a new product to build. A BOT is one small text file. Same code, same server, same login — a different name, a different colour, and a different starting point. That is the whole trick.

{
  "name": "TrustSquare Collector",
  "short_name": "Collector",
  "start_url": "/?bot=collector",
  "display": "standalone",
  "theme_color": "#C98A2E",
  "icons": [ ...same shape, bronze instead of green... ]
}

Point a second file at the same app with a different starting address and you have a second icon on the phone. A third gives you a third. They share one login, one listing database, one Trust Score — because they are the app.

What one wish costs, now that voice is free

Last night’s costing assumed voice-in would be a real bill. It is not. The phone does the listening and the speaking; we pay only for the thinking.

Part of a spoken wishWho does itCost
Hearing what you saidThe phone$0
Working out what you meantOur AI, one short call$0.003 – $0.01
Finding the matching listingsOur own database$0
Saying the answer aloudThe phone$0

A back-and-forth conversation is three or four of those calls, so about three cents for a whole conversation at the pessimistic price. The numbers in last night’s cost paper stand — a BOT conversation is a handful of wishes, not a new category of spending.

The one thing that would change this: a genie whose voice is a bought voice rather than the phone’s own. That is a per-word bill and it is a decision, not a detail. The prototype uses the free phone voice.

Why the Seller BOT is the one that earns money

The Collector BOT is the one that shows the magic — it is the demo, and it is what you asked for first, so it is what I built. But the number that is currently zero is people publishing a listing. A green “Sell” icon that a person talks to for ninety seconds and ends up with a live advert is the one that moves the business. I would build Collector to prove the idea and Seller to bank it.

Three things I would push back on

1 · An icon is expensive real estate. Nobody puts six of your icons on their phone. One or two, for the thing they actually do. So BOTs are not a menu we ship — they are offered to a person once we know what they are: the collector who has searched cards four times gets offered the bronze icon; the estate agent gets the blue one. That makes each icon feel personal, which is your “pre-tailored” instinct done properly.

2 · “Shall I search online?” needs care. It is a lovely moment and it is in the prototype. But TrustSquare introduces people; it is never the shop. A BOT that goes and finds the card on another site has to hand that person out, and there is no introduction fee in that. My reading: an outside search is fine as a favour that builds trust, and better still it becomes a want — he records what you are hunting and tells you the day a seller lists one. That keeps the value inside the marketplace instead of donating it to someone else’s checkout.

3 · The genie must never invent stock. If there are no Alpha Serra Angels, he says so plainly. A conversational interface that softens a “no” into a maybe is how people stop trusting it, and trust is the whole product.

Where this stands

Nothing is wired into the app. No flag, no deploy, nothing touching the launch. This is a working picture of the idea, in the genie sub-project, so it is ready when you are.

The two live-site gaps above are the exception worth thinking about — the iPhone full-screen tags are two lines, they make the existing app feel like an app, and they are worth doing whether or not BOTs ever happen.
