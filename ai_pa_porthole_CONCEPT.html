The Porthole — one question in front of the whole app

A working prototype of David's idea: the complex app stays exactly as it is, and a single round window with one question sits in front of it. Ask in your own words, answer up to four short questions, and land on something real — a journey laid out in legs, a guided listing, a free heritage read, or an introduction. The rules it must not break

No money for the trip passes through us. The porthole ends by handing the plan to a travel agency. That handover is the introduction — it is the whole business, not a step towards booking.
Fixed prices only. The dossier is 5 Tuppence whether the trip costs R20 000 or R2 million, and the price is on the button before anyone presses it.
Pre-information stays free. Heritage, routes, distances and what a thing roughly costs are free to read. Only the AI work that produces something is priced.
Selling stays free to try. Listing a B&B or a tour costs nothing; the Tuppence is paid when somebody actually asks to be introduced.
The app does not shrink. Anyone who wants the categories, the filters and the browse grid taps once and is back in them.

The one thing worth deciding early

How far the porthole may go before it hands over. Everything else follows from it. Two honest options:

Narrow. The porthole only routes — it reads the sentence and drops the person into an existing screen with the answers already filled in. Cheap, fast, no AI cost per use, and it can ship in days. It cannot answer a sentence about a place we have not built.

Wide. The porthole also composes — it writes a route we do not have, on the spot. That is the version that answers anything, and it is the one that costs money per question and needs the cap.

My call as it stands: build narrow first, because the narrow version already covers every example you listed except "anything" — a train trip, a B&B, a tour, a heritage site, shark diving, a holiday — and it proves whether people actually use the window before you pay per question for the version that answers anything. Wide is a switch you flip later on the same screen, not a rebuild.
